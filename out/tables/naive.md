# Naive comparison (M6b)

Computed the obvious way, the Not Helpful minus NMR gap at 30 days is **-21.1 pp**. With the day-7 landmark (M6a) it is **-15.0 pp**. The naive specification moves the headline number by -6.1 pp, overstating the association.

## What changes

Two things, and only two:

1. The first note is labelled with the verdict it eventually received, even if that
   arrived months later. In M6a a verdict after day 7 counts as NMR, because at
   day 7 it had not happened.
2. Authors who wrote again within 7 days are kept. M6a drops them — no feedback
   could have reached them, so their return is not a response to one.

Censoring is identical in both, so the difference below is the landmark design and
nothing else. The naive sample is 317,923 authors at 30 days against 227,324 in M6a; the extra 90,599 are the early returners.

## Why the naive NMR group is different

It is not a clean comparison group. It absorbs every note whose verdict never came,
which skews towards recent notes, and it keeps the authors who were already writing
again within the week — the most engaged people in the sample — without asking
whether any feedback had reached them.
